- **P9 — P:** Caloric helped close phlogiston-era combustion puzzles but opened the question of heat’s nature, later closed by thermodynamics.

## PART 2: EVIDENCE LINES
**T1 · Caloric theory of heat**
*   **P1 (P):** Lavoisier's _Traité_ (1789) said the heat and light of burning came from the caloric in oxygen gas. That took over phlogiston's job, but caloric mostly reorganized existing ideas about heat as a fluid. It didn't make any question unthinkable.
*   **P2 (P):** It put sensible heat, Black's latent heat (c. 1761), the gaseous state and expansion under one fluid. Laplace's correction to Newton's speed of sound (1816) extended it to sound. But heat and motion stayed separate.
*   **P3 (F):** Its constants were properties of particular materials: Black's specific and latent heats, Regnault's gas coefficients (1840s). Carnot's universal temperature function (1824) wasn't recognized as a constant. It later became Kelvin's 1/T.
*   **P4 (P):** Carnot's _Réflexions_ (1824) worked out the maximum efficiency of heat engines from existing engine practice, with almost no new data. James Thomson predicted in 1849 that pressure lowers the melting point of ice, and William Thomson confirmed it in 1850. That promise paid off within a year, not over decades.
*   **P5 (F):** Caloric rejected the earlier theories of heat as motion (Bacon, Boyle, Locke) instead of treating them as special cases. Rumford's cannon-boring paper (1798) was an attack on caloric, and caloric never absorbed it.
*   **P6 (F):** Caloric's novelty was a claim about what heat is, not a new reading of existing mathematics. Fourier's heat equation (1822) stayed deliberately neutral about caloric. So the math and its meaning separated _against_ caloric.
*   **P7 (F):** Lavoisier never held phlogiston deeply, and Black stayed cautious. Carnot's posthumous notes (published 1878) give up caloric, but that's a founder leaving the theory later, not paying a price to build it.
*   **P8 (S then D):** Laplace (1816), Carnot (1824) and Clapeyron (1834) were a real harvest. After Joule's paddle-wheel experiments (1845-49), the theory survived only through special fixes, as Thomson's 1849 "Account of Carnot's Theory" admits.
*   **P9 (P):** Carnot's reliance on caloric being conserved led to the Carnot-Joule conflict, which Clausius resolved in 1850. But caloric didn't close any deep earlier crisis.

**T2 · Darwinian evolution**
*   **P1 (S):** After the _Origin_ (1859), special creation and Paley-style design stopped working as scientific explanations.
*   **P2 (S):** Common descent tied together classification, the geography of species (the Beagle's Galápagos finches and mockingbirds, 1835), embryology, leftover organs and, in the _Descent of Man_ (1871), humans and animals.
*   **P3 (F):** No universal constant, and the _Origin_ has no quantitative law at all.
*   **P4 (S):** The argument rested mostly on data that already existed: breeders' records, Lyell's _Principles_ (1830-33), Malthus (read in 1838). Darwin's 1862 prediction of a very long-tongued moth for the orchid _Angraecum sesquipedale_ was confirmed by _Xanthopan morganii praedicta_ (1903).
*   **P5 (P):** Darwin explained why design _looked_ real and kept the Linnaean hierarchy as a family tree (_Origin_ ch. 13). But fixed species were refuted outright, not kept as a special case.
*   **P6 (P):** Blyth (1835) had described natural selection as a force that keeps species the same. Darwin read the same mechanism as a force that creates new ones. That's a reinterpretation, but of an argument, not of mathematics.
*   **P7 (S):** Darwin had admired Paley at Cambridge. He held back publication for about 20 years and wrote to Hooker in 1844 that admitting species change was "like confessing a murder."
*   **P8 (S):** Bates on mimicry (1862), Müller's _Für Darwin_ (1864), Haeckel's family trees (1866) and Wallace's biogeography, even while natural selection itself was out of favor later in the century.
*   **P9 (S):** It answered what Herschel called the "mystery of mysteries," then opened deeper problems: Jenkin's objection about blending inheritance (1867), Kelvin's estimate of the Earth's age, and where variation comes from.

**T3 · Ptolemaic epicycles**
*   **P1 (P):** Ptolemy's equant (_Almagest_ IX-XI) dropped the rule that planets move uniformly around the center of their own circle. The two-sphere, Earth-centered cosmos stayed.
*   **P2 (P):** One set of geometric tools covered the Sun, Moon and planets, nested into one system in the _Planetary Hypotheses_. The split between heavens and Earth stayed.
*   **P3 (F):** Its numbers were model settings, not universal constants: precession at 1° per century, epicycle ratios.
*   **P4 (P):** Ptolemy built mostly on Hipparchus and Babylonian records, with few new observations (R. R. Newton argued in 1977 that some were fitted). No predictions were confirmed long afterward.
*   **P5 (P):** The _Almagest_ absorbed Hipparchus's Sun and Moon models and Apollonius's result that an off-center circle and an epicycle give the same motion. That's incorporation, not turning a predecessor into a special case.
*   **P6 (P):** Within the tradition, the Maragha astronomers' Tusi couple (1247) and Ibn al-Shatir kept Ptolemy's predictions but changed their physical meaning. Osiander's preface (1543) made the "just a calculating tool" reading explicit. But Ptolemy's own contribution wasn't a reinterpretation.
*   **P7 (P):** Ptolemy admitted that the equant broke the uniform-circular-motion principle he himself held (_Almagest_ IX.2).
*   **P8 (S then D):** Productive from al-Battani through Maragha. Then the Alfonsine Tables (1270s) drifted, and Copernicus (1543) called the system a "monster."
*   **P9 (P):** It left the equant problem and the question of planetary distances to Maragha and Copernicus. It closed no earlier crisis of similar depth.

**T4 · Fixity of species**
*   **P1 (P):** Cuvier's 1796 memoir on living and fossil elephants established extinction. That killed the "great chain of being" assumption that no kind of creature is ever lost.
*   **P2 (P):** Linnaeus's binomial naming (1753/1758) unified the naming of all life, and Cuvier's correlation of parts unified anatomy. Both treated separate kinds as bedrock.
*   **P3 (F):** No constant of any kind.
*   **P4 (P):** Cuvier predicted marsupial pelvic bones from the jaw of the Montmartre opossum fossil (1804) and then uncovered them in public. Confirmed in hours, not decades.
*   **P5 (F):** It restated Aristotle's fixed essences. It didn't turn any predecessor into a special case.
*   **P6 (F):** No mathematics, and no shared content given a new meaning.
*   **P7 (P):** Linnaeus's _Peloria_ (1744) and his later ideas about hybrid origins forced him to soften "no new species."
*   **P8 (S then D):** Linnaean taxonomy and Cuvier's comparative anatomy delivered a lot. Then d'Orbigny's 27 successive creations (1849-52) and Agassiz's ever-growing list of creations showed rising cost per result.
*   **P9 (P):** Extinction plus replacement (Cuvier's _Discours_, 1812) opened the question of where new animals come from, which came up again in the Cuvier-Geoffroy debate (1830).

**T5 · Germ theory**
*   **P1 (S):** Pasteur's swan-neck flasks and his 1864 Sorbonne lecture made spontaneous generation untenable. The idea that specific diseases have specific causes pushed out vague "constitutional" causes.
*   **P2 (S):** Fermentation (1857), rotting, wound infection (Lister, 1867) and contagious disease turned out to be one thing: microbes at work.
*   **P3 (F):** No constant.
*   **P4 (F):** It ran on new instruments and new findings: Koch's plate cultures and stains, the tuberculosis bacterium (1882), the cholera bacterium (1884).
*   **P5 (P):** It explained why sanitation and Snow's removal of the Broad Street pump handle (1854) had worked. But miasma wasn't formally kept as a special case.
*   **P6 (F):** Fracastoro (1546) and Henle (1840) had the idea earlier. With no shared mathematics, the breakthrough wasn't a reinterpretation.
*   **P7 (F):** The costs fell on opponents: Pettenkofer drank a cholera culture in 1892 to disprove Koch. Pasteur and Koch lost nothing they held.
*   **P8 (S):** Anthrax vaccine (1881), rabies vaccine (1885) and Behring and Kitasato's diphtheria antitoxin (1890), leading to immunology.
*   **P9 (S):** Ivanovsky (1892) and Beijerinck (1898) found infectious agents that passed through filters and couldn't be grown, and healthy carriers like Mary Mallon (1907) raised the question of what the host contributes.

**T6 · Luminiferous ether**
*   **P1 (P):** Fresnel's wave optics (1818, with Arago's bright spot) ended the particle theory of light. Field theory weakened action at a distance. But the ether itself added something rather than removing it.
*   **P2 (S):** Maxwell (1862 and 1865) concluded that light and electromagnetism are waves in "the same medium."
*   **P3 (P):** The Weber-Kohlrausch ratio of electrical units (1856) came out at about the speed of light. It was treated as a property of the medium, not a universal limit.
*   **P4 (S):** Maxwell derived electromagnetic waves from existing measurements, and Hertz confirmed them in 1887-88.
*   **P5 (S):** Wave optics kept ray optics as the short-wavelength limit (Kirchhoff, 1882).
*   **P6 (P):** Fresnel's partial-drag coefficient (confirmed by Fizeau, 1851) later got two new meanings: Lorentz's local time, and Laue's reading as relativistic velocity addition (1907). The ether's equations outlived their meaning, but that isn't how the ether was founded.
*   **P7 (P):** Maxwell dropped his own 1861 model of gears and vortices by 1864. Lorentz later stripped the ether of all its mechanical properties.
*   **P8 (S then D):** A big harvest through Hertz. Then Michelson-Morley's null result (1887) forced FitzGerald's length contraction (1889) and Lorentz's 1895/1904 patches.
*   **P9 (S):** It settled what light is, then passed the null-result crisis to Einstein in 1905.

**T7 · Miasma theory**
*   **P1 (P):** The Hippocratic _On the Sacred Disease_ removed gods as a cause of epidemics. That deletion belongs to Greek naturalism generally as much as to miasma.
*   **P2 (P):** Sydenham's "epidemic constitutions" (1670s) and Farr's "zymotic" category (1842) grouped diseases together by blurring distinctions, not by showing the distinctions were false.
*   **P3 (F):** No constant.
*   **P4 (F):** Chadwick's sanitary report (1842) and Farr's finding that cholera deaths tracked elevation (1852) were survey results. No promise was ever confirmed.

## PART 3: POINT NOTES
*   **P3 only works for physics.** It automatically fails T2, T4, T5, T7 and T9, so it measures the discipline, not depth. Newton (T8) also shows G was installed about a century after the founder. Carnot's temperature function (T1) was a universal quantity that nobody recognized as a constant at the time.
*   **P4 bundles two separate tests.** "Built from old data" and "promises confirmed decades later" come apart: QM needed lots of new data but had long-delayed confirmations, and Cuvier worked from old data but was confirmed in hours. The point also doesn't say whether a founder's own new observations (Darwin's Beagle and barnacle work) count as "new data."
*   **P5 only works if the theory has mathematics.** Darwin, germ theory and miasma can at best explain why the old view seemed to work. There's no rule for how much that counts compared with a true limiting case. Clausius's treatment of Carnot (T12) sits right on that line.
*   **P6 also needs mathematics.** And it doesn't say whether the reinterpretation has to be the theory's own or can be done to it later by others. Phlogiston, the ether and the Maragha astronomers are all cases of the second kind.
*   **P7 has no rule for timing or who pays.** A founder can pay to build the theory (Newton, Planck), leave it later (Carnot, Farr), or resist the next theory (Einstein, Priestley). The definition's own Einstein example mixes the first and third. I only counted costs paid to build or consolidate the theory.
*   **P8 has no time window.** The scope limits (T2 stops before the modern synthesis, T12 stops at 1900) decide whether a theory gets S or "S then D." Every refuted theory came out "S then D," so the trend tracks later refutation more than depth at birth.
*   **P9 doesn't separate a deep open problem from a fatal anomaly.** Phlogiston's weight gain and the ether's null result both "handed successors a deeper floor," but only by getting replaced. I gave the ether S and phlogiston P because the ether first closed a real crisis (what light is). That call is contestable.
*   **P1 depends on whose deletion it is.** Hippocratic naturalism (T7) and Cuvier's extinction (T4) are real deletions, carried by frameworks that weren't deep breakthroughs.
*   **Overall, the instrument risks being circular.** Most of the definitions are written around Newton, Einstein and Planck, so those theories score near-perfect partly by construction. The most useful points across disciplines were P1, P2, the trend half of P8, and P9.

### PART 2 — EVIDENCE LINES

**T1 · The caloric theory of heat**
*   **P1**: Lavoisier’s *Traité élémentaire de chimie* (1789) added the new element "calorique" to explain heat, rather than deleting an established paradigm.
*   **P2**: Joseph Black’s calorimetry (c. 1760) partially unified the distinct phenomena of latent heat and specific heat under the behavior of a single self-repelling fluid.
*   **P3**: Neither Lavoisier nor Carnot installed a fundamental universal scaling constant comparable to $c$ or $h$.
*   **P4**: The theory was built empirically *a posteriori* from Black's ice-calorimetry data and Carnot's observations of working steam engines.
*   **P5**: Caloric did not conservatively embed older mathematical mechanics as a limiting case; it simply stood apart as a fluid theory.
*   **P6**: Caloric mathematically described the conservation of a fluid, and its formalism meant exactly what it said without splitting from a prior interpretation.
*   **P7**: Lavoisier enthusiastically championed his new caloric framework without having to sacrifice a deeply held prior personal axiom.
*   **P8**: It generated highly successful precision thermometry (Regnault) and Carnot's engine analysis (1824), but degenerated rapidly when confronted with Joule's mechanical equivalent of heat (1843).
*   **P9**: Carnot's paradox regarding how heat could produce work merely by dropping in temperature left a theoretical wound that paved the way for thermodynamics.

**T2 · Darwinian evolution by natural selection**
*   **P1**: The *Origin of Species* (1859) decisively deleted the necessity of Paley's teleological "watchmaker" design from biology.
*   **P2**: Darwin unified completely disparate fields, demonstrating that biogeography (Galápagos finches) and comparative anatomy (homologous bone structures) were artifacts of common descent.
*   **P3**: The theory of natural selection introduced no universal mathematical constant.
*   **P4**: Darwin formulated the theory via massive empirical induction after observing species variation during the *Beagle* voyage (1831–1836), not via mathematical derivation.
*   **P5**: Natural selection did not demote Cuvier’s catastrophism or Linnaean fixity to a limiting case; it completely destroyed them.
*   **P6**: The theory was built on qualitative reasoning and natural history, utilizing no mathematical formalism that separated from its meaning.
*   **P7**: Darwin suffered deeply over the implications of his theory, delaying publication for decades and confessing to Hooker in 1844 that admitting species change was "like confessing a murder."
*   **P8**: The theory generated massive biological research programs, and despite the temporary "eclipse of Darwinism" around 1900, it saturated biology completely following the modern synthesis.
*   **P9**: It brilliantly solved adaptation but left the exact mechanism of inheritance completely open (exacerbated by Fleeming Jenkin’s 1867 swamping argument), a crisis resolved only by later genetics.

*   **P9**: The physical absurdity of the equant (uniform angular motion around an empty point) left a theoretical wound that motivated Islamic astronomers (al-Tusi) and eventually Copernicus to seek a new system.
