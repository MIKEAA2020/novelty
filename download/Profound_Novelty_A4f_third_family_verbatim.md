## PART 2: EVIDENCE LINES

### T1: Caloric theory of heat
- **P1 (P):** Black's c. 1760 separation of quantity of heat from temperature (latent heat of ice; the Fahrenheit–Boerhaave mercury-water mixing anomaly) ended the equation of thermometer reading with heat quantity, but heat-as-motion stayed thinkable (Rumford's cannon-boring 1798, Davy's ice-rubbing 1799).
- **P2 (P):** The Lavoisier–Laplace ice calorimeter (Mémoire sur la chaleur, 1783) put combustion, animal respiration and specific heats on one scale, and Laplace's 1816 adiabatic correction of Newton's sound-speed formula tied acoustics to heat, but no deep ancient distinction fell.
- **P3 (F):** Specific and latent heats are substance-specific, Dulong–Petit (1819) was an empirical regularity, and Carnot's 1824 "function of temperature" was an undetermined function, not a constant that re-scaled anything.
- **P4 (P):** The fluid picture was built to save Black's and Lavoisier–Laplace's calorimetric data, yet it issued honored notes: Laplace's 1816 sound-speed correction (borne out by Moll and van Beek's 1820s measurements) and James Thomson's 1849 Carnot–Clapeyron inference that ice melts lower under pressure, confirmed by William Thomson in 1850.
- **P5 (P):** Black showed the Boerhaave–Fahrenheit mixing rule holds only for equal specific heats, explaining why it worked for like substances and failed for mercury and water, but caloric never recovered a predecessor theory as a limit.
- **P6 (P):** Fourier's 1822 Théorie analytique de la chaleur declined any hypothesis about heat's nature and outlived the fluid, and Clausius's 1850 paper reused Clapeyron's 1834 form of Carnot's cycle with heat no longer conserved, but caloric's own novelty was not a rereading of held equations.
- **P7 (F):** Lavoisier and Laplace's 1783 Mémoire explicitly left open whether heat is a fluid or particle vis viva, so installing caloric cost its makers no held commitment; the costly moments (Carnot's private abandonment in notes of c. 1830–32, Thomson's 1849–51 struggle) came afterward.
- **P8 (S then D):** The harvest was Laplace 1816, Poisson's 1823 adiabatic law, Carnot 1824 and Clapeyron 1834; degeneration set in as Rumford/Davy anomalies, Regnault's 1840s gas measurements and Joule's 1843–49 experiments demanded ever more patching.
- **P9 (S):** Carnot's 1824 Réflexions, resting on conserved caloric, left open how heat yields work; Thomson's 1848–51 papers confronted it with Joule's conversion and Clausius's 1850 paper founded the second law on that conflict.

### T2: Epicycle tradition (Ptolemaic astronomy)
- **P1 (F):** Amico's De motibus (1536), Fracastoro's Homocentrica (1538) and al-Bitruji's 12th-century Andalusian system show that homocentric spheres never became unthinkable after the Almagest.
- **P2 (P):** Almagest IV–XIII treats Sun, Moon and five planets with one deferent–epicycle apparatus, and XII.1 (Apollonius's theorem) shows eccentric and epicycle equivalent, dissolving retrogression into circular motion, but no celestial/terrestrial-type divide fell.
- **P3 (F):** Each planet had its own fitted radii, periods and eccentricities, and the one shared parameter, precession of 1° per century (Almagest VII.2), was inherited from Hipparchus and rescaled nothing.
- **P4 (F):** Ptolemy's models were fitted to observation (the lunar evection was found from quadrature data, Almagest V.3) and issued no honored novel forecast; the Alfonsine Tables (c. 1272) drifted from the sky by the 1500s.
- **P5 (P):** Almagest V.2–5 keeps Hipparchus's lunar model as the syzygy case and adds the evection for quadratures, but the Eudoxan–Aristotelian homocentric spheres were replaced outright and the equant (IX.2) relaxed uniformity about the center.
- **P6 (P):** Copernicus (1543) reread the Ptolemaic outer-planet epicycle as Earth's annual orbit, Tycho's system is geometrically equivalent, and Osiander's unsigned preface and Ptolemy's Planetary Hypotheses split saving-geometry from physical claim, but the Almagest's own novelty was not a rereading.
- **P7 (P):** The equant gave up uniform motion about the deferent's center, and Ptolemy defends complex celestial models in XIII.2, but no record shows a founder paying a personal cost; the equant was attacked by Ibn al-Haytham's Shukuk (c. 1025) and Copernicus's Commentariolus.
- **P8 (S then D):** The harvest included the Alfonsine Tables, Ibn Yunus's Hakimi Zij, the Maragha Tusi couple and Urdi lemma (used by Copernicus), and Ibn al-Shatir's models; degeneration showed when Tycho's 1563 Jupiter–Saturn conjunction found the Alfonsine tables a month off.
- **P9 (S):** Ibn al-Haytham's Shukuk exposed the clash between equant and uniform circular motion, Maragha astronomers built the Tusi couple to repair it, Copernicus cited the equant as motive, and Kepler's Astronomia nova (1609), from Tycho's Mars data, took the problem to ellipses.

### T3: Germ theory of disease
- **P1 (S):** Pasteur's swan-neck flasks (1859–62; Sorbonne lecture 1864) and Tyndall's 1876–77 spore work ended live spontaneous generation, and Cohn's 1872 species classification with Koch's 1881 pure-culture method removed Billroth-style pleomorphism (Coccobacteria septica, 1874).
- **P2 (S):** Pasteur's 1857 lactic-fermentation memoir and Études sur la bière (1876) made fermentation and putrefaction the work of organisms, and his 1878 germ-theory lecture with Joubert's anthrax work carried it to infection, removing the Liebig-era wall between chemical decay and disease; Lister's 1867 Lancet antisepsis applied it to wounds.
- **P3 (F):** Nothing in the theory is a universal constant; Koch's postulates (Löffler 1883, Koch 1884) are a criterion, not a number.
- **P4 (P):** It issued honored notes (Lister 1867 antisepsis, Pouilly-le-Fort anthrax vaccine trial 1881, Manson's 1878 mosquito–filaria hypothesis bearing fruit in Ross's 1897 malaria work), but it was not over-determined by old data, since it needed new microscopy, gelatin media (1881) and staining.
- **P5 (S):** Chadwick's 1842 sanitary report and Snow's 1854 Broad Street pump showed sanitation worked, and Koch's 1883–84 cholera vibrio and the 1892 Hamburg (unfiltered) versus Altona (filtered) water contrast explained why, recovering contagionist quarantine as the special case for specific agents.
- **P6 (F):** Germ theory had no mathematical formalism that could precede its meaning; Koch's postulates were criteria, not equations.
- **P7 (F):** The costly stands were opponents' (Pettenkofer swallowing Koch's cholera culture in 1892, Liebig, Pouchet), and Pasteur and Koch confirmed rather than surrendered their convictions; Koch's later softening of the postulates for carriers (cholera 1893) was a correction, not a founding cost.
- **P8 (S):** Behring–Kitasato diphtheria antitoxin (1890), Pasteur's rabies vaccine (1885), Ehrlich's Salvarsan (1909), Fleming's penicillin (1928), Domagk's Prontosil (1935) and modern virology and microbiome science are harvests nobody ordered in 1876, and it is still generating.
- **P9 (S):** Koch's cholera carriers (1893) and Typhoid Mary (1907) broke one-microbe-one-illness, Ivanovsky (1892) and Beijerinck (1898) found filterable agents that could not be cultured, and the Metchnikoff (phagocytosis, 1884) versus Behring (humoral, 1890) split opened immunology.

### T4: Luminiferous ether
- **P1 (P):** Young (1801), Fresnel (1818–21) and Foucault's 1850 finding that light is slower in water made Newtonian emission optics unthinkable, but the ether itself added a plenum rather than deleting anything, and action at a distance stayed alive in Weber's 1846 electrodynamics.
- **P2 (S):** Maxwell's 1861–62 "On Physical Lines of Force" used the Weber–Kohlrausch electrical ratio (1856), matching Fizeau's light speed (1849), to identify light as transverse undulation of the same medium that carries electric and magnetic action.
- **P3 (P):** The Weber–Kohlrausch constant, identified with light speed by Maxwell, became the wave speed of the medium, but as a property of the ether and relative to it, not a universal cap until Einstein's 1905.
- **P4 (P):** The wave and field programs made honored derivation-first forecasts (Poisson's bright spot confirmed by Arago 1819, Hamilton's conical refraction 1832 confirmed by Lloyd 1833, Maxwell's 1865 waves detected by Hertz 1887–88), but the ether's own distinctive forecast, an ether wind, returned null in Michelson–Morley 1887 and was rescued ad hoc (FitzGerald–Lorentz contraction, 1889/1892).
- **P5 (S):** Fresnel's 1818 diffraction memoir recovered rectilinear propagation as the short-wavelength limit of wave superposition, and Maxwell's 1865 Dynamical Theory recovered Coulomb, Ampère and Faraday induction as special cases.
- **P6 (P):** Hertz (1890) identified Maxwell's theory with the equations alone, dropping the mechanical ether model, and Lorentz's 1895 theory kept the equations with the ether as a bare rest frame, so formalism outlived meaning, but the ether program's novelty was not a rereading.
- **P7 (P):** Young's 1817 letter to Arago on transverse vibrations and Fresnel's 1821–23 adoption forced a fluid-like, sound-like ether to become elastic-solid-like, and Maxwell dropped his 1861 vortex model in 1865, but no founder's deepest commitment was sacrificed, and Kelvin held the ether until his death (1907).
- **P8 (S then D):** The harvest was interference and polarization optics, Faraday–Maxwell field theory, Hertz's radio waves and Lorentz's 1896–97 electron theory; degeneration came with Stokes's 1845 ether drag, Fresnel's partial drag (Fizeau 1851), FitzGerald–Lorentz contraction, Lorentz's 1895 local time and 1904 transformations, and Kelvin's 1900 "two clouds" lecture.
- **P9 (S):** Transverse waves forced a solid ether (Fresnel 1821), Maxwell closed light–electromagnetism but exposed absolute motion relative to the ether, and Michelson–Morley 1887 and Kelvin's 1900 clouds handed relativity and quantum theory their founding problems.

### T5: Miasma theory of disease
- **P1 (P):** The Hippocratic Airs, Waters, Places moved epidemic causes into the environment and away from the divine, but miasma never made rivals unthinkable, since Fracastoro's De contagione (1546) and Henle's 1840 Von den Miasmen und Kontagien kept contagion alive.
- **P2 (P):** Chadwick's 1842 Sanitary Report and Farr's 1852 elevation–cholera-mortality tables bound filth, smell, drainage, typhus, typhoid and cholera into one environmental cause, an explanatory unification rather than the dissolving of a deep distinction.
- **P3 (F):** No constant appears; Farr's 1852 elevation law was an empirical mortality-by-altitude relation.
- **P4 (P):** Nightingale's 1855 Sanitary Commission clean-up of Scutari was followed by a steep mortality fall, and Bazalgette's London sewers (1858–75) accompanied declining cholera, but Farr's own 1852 elevation law was overturned by Snow's 1854 water-source evidence, and the doctrine was not derived from old data by a new theory.
- **P5 (P):** Pettenkofer's x–y–z scheme (1850s–60s) made a specific germ necessary but not sufficient, alongside soil-water conditions and individual disposition, partly absorbing contagionism, but it never recovered a displaced theory as a limit with a stated failure condition.
- **P6 (F):** There was no mathematical formalism whose meaning could be reread; Farr's elevation law was a tabulated empirical regularity.
- **P7 (F):** No founder of the doctrine is recorded surrendering a cherished commitment to install it; the documented costs (Farr's 1866 concession to Snow's water evidence, Pettenkofer's 1892 self-experiment) came from defending it.
- **P8 (S then D):** The harvest (Public Health Act 1848, General Register Office vital statistics, Bazalgette's sewers, Nightingale's 1858 polar-area diagrams, Pettenkofer's Munich hygiene institute 1879) accrued regardless of the doctrine's truth, while the doctrine itself degenerated into ad hoc rescue (Pettenkofer's y and z) after Snow 1854 and Koch 1883–84.
- **P9 (P):** Miasma left "what is the miasma?" open and Farr's 1839 zymotic classification and Henle's 1840 essay carried it toward living contagia, but the specificity problem pre-dated the doctrine (Fracastoro 1546) and was answered by discarding the concept, not by a re-founded floor.

### T6: Newtonian mechanics
- **P1 (S):** Law 1 and Book III Prop 4 (the Moon test) abolished the sublunary/celestial divide and Aristotelian natural places, and the Book II §9 scholium refuted Cartesian vortices; "what keeps a projectile moving?" lost its point.
- **P2 (S):** Book III Prop 4 shows the Moon's fall follows the same inverse-square gravity as terrestrial fall, and Props 24 (tides) and 41 (the comet of 1680) bring seas and comets under the same force.
- **P3 (P):** The Principia states gravitation as proportional to the product of masses over distance squared (Book III Props 7–8) in proportions with no constant; G was implicit in Cavendish's 1798 density-of-the-Earth measurement and named later, so the 1687 installation of G is partly anachronistic.
- **P4 (S):** Newton derived the inverse-square law from Kepler's third law and the ellipse from it (De motu 1684; Book I Props 1, 11) using Picard's 1670 Earth measurement; notes honored: Halley's 1705 comet forecast (return Dec 1758), Earth's oblateness (Lapland arc 1736–37), Neptune (1846).
- **P5 (S):** Book I Props 1 and 11–13 derive Kepler's area law and ellipses from central force, Book III shows Kepler's laws hold only approximately (Sun's motion about the barycenter, mutual perturbations), and Galileo's uniform g appears as the small-height limit, although Cartesian vortices were refuted outright.
- **P6 (P):** Kepler's laws (1609/1619), Huygens's centrifugal formula (1673) and the Hooke–Wren–Halley inverse-square inference from circular orbits (1679–84) preceded Newton, and reading them as one universal force was the leap, but Newton also had to invent new mathematics (Book I Lemmas I–XI).
- **P7 (S):** Newton told Bentley (25 Feb 1693) that action at a distance without a medium was an absurdity, yet the Principia's gravity worked that way, and Leibniz (Leibniz–Clarke correspondence, 1715–16) and Huygens attacked it as occult; Newton answered in the 1713 General Scholium.
- **P8 (S):** Clairaut's 1749 lunar-apse resolution, Laplace's Mécanique céleste (1799–1825), Neptune (Le Verrier and Adams, 1846), Hamiltonian mechanics (1833) and Poincaré's chaos (1890) followed, with dynamical systems and orbital mechanics still going (Vulcan, 1859, was a failed harvest).
- **P9 (S):** The General Scholium (1713, hypotheses non fingo) and the Bentley letters left gravity's mechanism open, alongside absolute space (Leibniz–Clarke, Mach 1883) and Mercury's 43″ anomaly (Le Verrier 1859), all unresolved until Einstein's 1915 field equations.

### T7: Phlogiston chemistry
- **P1 (F):** Jean Rey (1630) and Boyle (New Experiments to Make Fire and Flame Stable and Ponderable, 1673) had already ascribed calx weight gain to added matter and that view stayed thinkable (Lavoisier revived it), so Stahl's 1703 loss-of-phlogiston story deleted no question.
- **P2 (S):** Stahl's Specimen Becherianum (1703) read burning, metal calcination and charcoal reduction of calx as one process of phlogiston loss and gain, and Cavendish's 1766 "inflammable air" and Priestley's respiration work extended it, dissolving the burning/roasting divide.
- **P3 (F):** The theory was qualitative and its one quantitative puzzle, phlogiston's weight, stayed unsettled (Guyton de Morveau's 1772 "levity" proposal).
- **P4 (F):** Phlogiston was built to save known operations and met calx weight gain with patches, and oxygen (Scheele 1771–72, Priestley 1774) was found by experiment, not derived from the theory.
- **P5 (P):** Stahl retained and renamed Becher's terra pinguis from Physica subterranea (1669), and Kirwan's 1784 phlogiston-as-inflammable-air let phlogistic accounts be partly translated into oxidation–reduction ones, but no predecessor was recovered as a limit with a failure condition.
- **P6 (F):** There was no mathematical formalism; the Lavoisier–Priestley dispute over the same weights (Réflexions sur le phlogistique, 1783; dephlogisticated air, 1774) is a reinterpretation of experiments, not equations, so it fails the point as defined.
- **P7 (F):** Stahl's vitalist, anti-Boylean chemistry cost him nothing to hold, while the documented costly stands (Priestley's 1800 Doctrine of Phlogiston Established, Cavendish's and Kirwan's 1784 defenses) were resistance to Lavoisier.
- **P8 (S then D):** Black's fixed air (1754), Rutherford's nitrogen (1772), Scheele's fire air (1771–72), Cavendish's inflammable air (1766) and Priestley's oxygen (1774) came from phlogistic pneumatic chemistry, while negative-weight and buoyancy patches (Guyton 1772) and the 1780s rescues by Kirwan and Cavendish marked the decline against Lavoisier's balance accounting.
- **P9 (S):** Unifying combustion and calcination sharpened the weight-gain problem and multiplied the "airs," and Lavoisier's sealed note of November 1772 on sulfur and phosphorus gaining weight took Stahl's unsolved problem as his founding problem, leading to the oxygen theory (1777–83).

### T8: Quantum mechanics
- **P1 (S):** Heisenberg's 1925 paper banned unobservable electron orbits, the 1927 uncertainty relations denied simultaneous exact position and momentum, and Born's 1926 probability reading killed strict determinism, so "what path does the electron take between jumps?" died.
- **P2 (S):** De Broglie's 1924 thesis, Schrödinger's 1926 proof of the equivalence of wave and matrix mechanics, Dirac–Jordan transformation theory (1926–27) and Heitler–London's 1927 H₂ bond unified wave and particle, and physics and chemistry.
- **P3 (S):** Planck's 14 December 1900 talk installed h from the black-body fit (with k), the quantum of action that sets the scale of atoms and spectra.
- **P4 (S):** Planck's 1900 formula was fitted to the Rubens–Kurlbaum measurements and Heisenberg's 1925 paper reworked old spectral-intensity data with Kramers's dispersion formula; notes honored: photoelectric law (Millikan 1914–16), de Broglie waves (Davisson–Germer 1927), Bose–Einstein condensation (1924–25, observed 1995), Bell's 1964 inequalities (Freedman–Clauser 1972, Aspect 1982, loophole-free 2015).
- **P5 (S):** Bohr's correspondence principle (1913–20) and Ehrenfest's 1927 theorem (mean values obey Newton's equations) recover classical behavior at large quantum numbers and for wide wave packets.
- **P6 (S):** Heisenberg's 1925 title announces a reinterpretation (Umdeutung) of kinematics, and Schrödinger (1926) held the wave equation but read ψ as charge density until Born's June 1926 probability reading.
- **P7 (S):** Planck spent 1900 to c. 1914 trying to fit quanta into continuous absorption (his 1911 "second theory") and later described the hypothesis as an act of desperation; Einstein rejected Born's indeterminism (letter of 4 December 1926) and pressed EPR (1935); Schrödinger recoiled from quantum jumps at Copenhagen (1926).
- **P8 (S):** The transistor (1947), Townes's maser (1954), Maiman's laser (1960), Pauling's chemical-bond theory (1931), Dirac's QED (1927–28), Shor's algorithm (1994) and Bennett–Brassard cryptography (1984) form a harvest still expanding.
- **P9 (S):** Von Neumann's 1932 analysis of the measurement cut, Schrödinger's cat and EPR (both 1935) and Bell's 1964 theorem left the measurement problem open, and quantum theory's clash with gravity is the deeper floor.

### T9: Relativity
- **P1 (S):** Einstein's 1905 §§1–2 made absolute simultaneity and the ether superfluous, and GR (1915) removed gravity as a force in flat space; "how fast are we moving through the ether?" died.
- **P2 (S):** Minkowski's 1908 "Space and Time" fused space and time, Einstein's September 1905 inertia-and-energy paper fused mass and energy, and GR fused gravity with geometry via the 1907 equivalence principle.
- **P3 (S):** The second postulate of 1905 made c an invariant universal speed limit, although c's value dated to Römer (1676), Fizeau (1849) and Maxwell (1861); the new thing was its role, and κ = 8πG/c⁴ carries it into GR.
- **P4 (S):** SR answered Michelson–Morley (1887) with no new experiments, and GR (November 1915) reproduced Mercury's 43″ per century (Le Verrier 1859) with no free parameter; notes honored: Eddington 1919, Pound–Rebka 1959, Shapiro 1964, LIGO's GW150914 (2015, a century after 1916), EHT 2019.
- **P5 (S):** SR recovers Newtonian kinematics for v ≪ c, and GR recovers Poisson's equation for weak, slow fields (Einstein's 1915–16 Newtonian-limit check), explaining why Newton worked and where it fails (Mercury).
- **P6 (S):** Lorentz's 1904 transformations and Poincaré's 1905–06 group analysis already held the equations, and Einstein's 1905 paper read them as the structure of time itself, making local time real and the ether superfluous.
- **P7 (S):** Einstein gave up the ether he had tried to detect in 1899–1901 and Newtonian time, then introduced the cosmological constant (1917) against an expanding universe, disbelieved Friedmann (1922) and Lemaître (1927), doubted gravitational waves (1936) and resisted quantum indeterminacy for thirty years.
- **P8 (S):** Friedmann (1922), Lemaître (1927) and Hubble (1929) led to big-bang cosmology, the CMB (Penzias–Wilson 1965), Kerr (1963), Penrose's 1965 singularity theorem, EHT (2019) and GPS clock corrections, all uninvited harvests.
- **P9 (S):** Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems (1965–70) and Hawking's 1974–76 radiation and information-loss problem set GR against quantum theory, joined by the cosmological-constant problem.

### T10: Thermodynamics
- **P1 (S):** Joule's paddle-wheel work (1845–49), Clausius (1850) and Thomson (1851) made a conserved heat-fluid and unlimited conversion of heat to work unthinkable in physics, though Maxwell's 1867 demon shows the second-law prohibition was later contested as thought experiment.
- **P2 (S):** Joule's mechanical equivalent (1843–49), Mayer (1842) and Helmholtz (1847) united heat, work, electricity and chemical action under energy conservation, and Clausius's entropy (1865) and Gibbs's 1875–78 "Equilibrium of Heterogeneous Substances" united chemistry with thermodynamics.
- **P3 (P):** Joule's equivalent (about 4.15 J/cal), Thomson's 1848 absolute scale (zero near −273 °C) and k (from Boltzmann's 1877 S = k ln W, named by Planck c. 1900) are universal, but J is a unit conversion after energy is defined and none re-scales the system as G, c and h do.
- **P4 (S):** Clausius (1850) derived the second law from Carnot's theorem plus Joule's equivalent, with Regnault's data and no new observation; notes honored: J. Thomson's 1849 ice forecast (1850), the steam-expansion forecast of Rankine and Clausius (1850, Hirn's experiments), Maxwell's 1860 density-independent gas viscosity (own experiments 1866), Boltzmann's 1884 derivation of Stefan's T⁴ law.
- **P5 (S):** Clausius (1850) and Thomson (1851) kept Carnot's theorem and caloric calorimetry while dropping conservation of heat, explaining why caloric worked (no net work conversion) and where it failed (Joule), and kinetic theory (Krönig 1856, Clausius 1857) recovers the ideal-gas law as a limit.
- **P6 (S):** Clausius (1850) reused Clapeyron's 1834 Carnot-cycle diagram with new meaning, and the entropy relation dS = δQ/T (1865) received its statistical meaning from Boltzmann's 1877 S = k ln W with the thermodynamic formalism untouched.
- **P7 (S):** Thomson's 1848 absolute scale rested on Carnot's caloric theory, which he gave up in 1849–51 after resisting Joule at the 1847 Oxford meeting, and Maxwell (1867 letter to Tait) and Boltzmann (1877 reply to Loschmidt) surrendered the second law's absolute status for a statistical one.
- **P8 (S):** Gibbs's chemical thermodynamics (1875–78), Van 't Hoff and Arrhenius solution theory (1884–87), Linde's refrigeration (1895), Nernst's heat theorem (1906), Shannon (1948), Landauer (1961), Bekenstein–Hawking black-hole entropy (1973–75) and today's fluctuation theorems keep coming.
- **P9 (S):** Loschmidt's 1876 reversibility objection and Zermelo's 1896 recurrence objection to Boltzmann's H-theorem (1872), Thomson's 1852 heat-death paper and the Rayleigh–Jeans equipartition failure (1900–05) opened the questions that led to quantum theory.

## PART 3: POINT NOTES

**Conventions I had to adopt (the definitions did not fix them):**
- P5 was coded in the direction "the theory recovers what it displaced as a limit or special case, with a stated failure condition." The reverse reading (the successor demotes the theory) would raise T1, T2, T4 and T7.
- P6 was restricted to mathematical or quantitative formalism: S = novelty chiefly a rereading of held formalism; P = the split is real and consequential in the theory's history but not the source of its novelty; F = none.
- P7: S = documented personal surrender by a founder; P = structural or collective cost without personal attestation; F = none.
- P9: S = the theory's own open problem was taken up by a successor as a founding problem; P = the problem pre-dated the theory or was only dissolved.

**P1 (deletion):** "Unthinkable" has no time or audience. T2 exposed it (homocentric spheres were revived in 1536–38 and by al-Bitruji), and T7 exposed it again (Rey and Boyle's weight-gain view stayed available). T3 raises attribution: spontaneous generation died from Pasteur and Tyndall's experiments as much as from the theory. T4 shows that "deletion" conflates deleting a rival with adding an entity.

**P2 (unification):** It does not separate true from false theories (T7 phlogiston and T4 ether score S), and it blurs ontological unification (Newton, Einstein) with explanatory or taxonomic unification (T5 miasma, T1). T4 also raises credit assignment: was unification the ether's or the field equations'?

**P3 (constant):** Effectively defined for physics only, so F on T3, T5 and T7 is inapplicability, not negative evidence. T6 exposed an anachronism: the Principia states proportions and G was named after Cavendish (1798), so the exemplar's "1687" installation is loose. T9 shows the opposite tension: c was old (1676–1861) and only its role was new, so "new constant" and "new role" are conflated. T10's J is a unit conversion, and T1's Carnot function is a function, not a constant.

**P4 (derivation-first):** It bundles two claims (over-determination by old data; honored long-range notes) that can split. T8 and T3 exposed this: quantum theory rested on decades of old anomalies but also needed decisive new data (Compton 1923, Davisson–Germer 1927); germ theory had honored notes (Lister, Ross) but needed heavy new observation. No time threshold for "honored decades later," and T5's outcome-level forecasts (Scutari 1855) blur "theory" and "policy." T4 again splits entity from program.

**P5 (conservative embedding):** The direction is undefined (see conventions), exposed by T1, T2, T7. The "limiting case" wording presumes mathematics, so T3 and T5 could only be coded on qualitative embedding. T6 exposed a second issue: Newton embedded Kepler and Galileo but refuted Descartes outright.

**P6 (formalism and meaning):** It presupposes a mathematical formalism, forcing F on T3, T5 and T7 even though Lavoisier's reinterpretation of Priestley's data has the same shape.
